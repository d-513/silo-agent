# Silo Wins the Boundary, Trails on Reach

As of mid-November 2026, Silo is ahead of all five rivals on the thing that is hardest to add later and behind them on most things that are easy to see. No other product in this set combines **self-hosting, real multi-user access control, an always-on per-Bot container, a TLS-inspecting egress gateway with surrogate secrets, and a prompt-injection filter on every tool result**. OpenClaw, Hermes Agent and OpenBot run on the host by default. Grok Bot puts every Bot a user owns on one shared VM and tells users not to treat Bots as a security boundary. Only Meta's Muse built a comparable boundary, and Muse is closed, hosted and single-user. The gaps sit in reach and in learning. Silo has 3 chat channels where OpenClaw has about 30 and Hermes about 28. It has no Slack or Teams, which 4 and 3 of the 5 rivals cover, no Android or desktop client, no push notifications, no Bot-to-Bot collaboration, no webhook triggers, no outbound phone calls, no password-manager or payment rail, and no agent that writes its own skills or learns a task from a demonstration. For a developer choosing what to build, the evidence points one way: build the reach features on top of the boundary Silo already has. Rivals have tried the reverse, bolting isolation onto a reach-first design, and their CVE lists and "not a security boundary" disclaimers show how that has gone.

## Five rivals, two shapes, and Silo between them

The rivals split cleanly by shape. **OpenClaw, Hermes Agent and OpenBot are self-hosted frameworks that run on the operator's own machine by default.** OpenClaw is the Node.js Gateway behind the most-starred agent project, with **about 395k GitHub stars** and v2026.11.4 shipped on 16 November ([GitHub](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw); [release notes](https://docs.openclaw.ai/releases/2026.11.4)). Hermes Agent is Nous Research's Python harness, at **264k stars** and v0.23.2 on 16 November ([GitHub](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent)). OpenBot, from Nightly Labs (Synthetify Labs), is a source-available Electron app that wraps vendors' coding-agent CLIs such as Codex and Claude Code. It has shipped 126 releases since 13 August, the latest 0.72.0 on 16 November ([changelog](https://openbot.run/changelog)), and it is not the CopilotKit or Intel project of the same name. **Grok Bot and Muse are hosted products that give each user a persistent cloud Linux computer.** Grok Bot is xAI's always-on Bot team on Cursor-run VMs, in beta from 11 August ([x.ai](https://x.ai/news/introducing-grok-bot)). Muse is Meta's consumer agent, announced 8 September, with one "Muse Secure VM" per user ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/)).

Silo belongs to neither group. It is self-hosted like the first three, but it runs as a server: one Go control plane holds every key and drives one durable, capability-narrowed container per Bot for many users, which makes it structurally closer to the hosted pair ([AGENTS.md § Processes and hops](../AGENTS.md)). That position is the strategic fact. The frameworks cannot easily become multi-tenant, and their own documentation says so. The hosted products cannot become self-hosted at all. ## Reach is the widest gap: channels, clients and phones

The clearest finding is that **Silo is the narrowest product here at the edges where people meet an agent**. Its three chat channels (Telegram, an unofficial linked-device WhatsApp, and Discord) compare with OpenClaw's bundled and official-plugin list. That list covers Slack, Microsoft Teams, Signal, iMessage, SMS, Matrix, Google Chat, LINE, Mattermost, IRC and more, and adds a Voice Call plugin for telephony through Plivo, Telnyx or Twilio ([OpenClaw docs: Channels](https://docs.openclaw.ai/channels)). Hermes runs about 28 platforms from one gateway, including Slack, Teams, Signal, SMS, Email, and iMessage via BlueBubbles or Photon ([Hermes messaging docs](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/user-guide/messaging/)). Even the young OpenBot answers in **Slack, Discord, Telegram and WhatsApp** ([OpenBot compare page](https://openbot.run/compare/openclaw)). Grok Bot aims at the workplace: Slack for Team Bots, Microsoft Teams since 1.0, X, an email address per account, and meetings it joins but does not yet speak in ([flaviocopes](https://flaviocopes.com/grok-bot/)). The single most widely held channel Silo lacks is **Slack, offered by four of five rivals**, and Teams follows at three. Silo's own self-assessment names these gaps ([TODO.md §8](../TODO.md)). OpenClaw also lets users answer approvals inside Discord, Telegram and Matrix with rich cards ([OpenClaw docs index](https://docs.openclaw.ai/llms.txt)). Silo's approvals live only in its web and iOS apps, which matters once a Bot is used mainly through a messenger.

Clients show the same pattern. Silo ships a web console and a SwiftUI iOS app with widgets, Live Activities and a share extension. It has **no Android app, no desktop app, no CLI and no push service**, so its iOS Live Activities go stale once the app is suspended ([AGENTS.md § iOS](../AGENTS.md)). The iOS calling stack (CallKit, the echo-cancelled audio engine, Silero VAD) "has not met real hardware" ([ios/todo_skipped.md](../ios/todo_skipped.md)). Every rival but Hermes, whose iOS/Android app is still a waitlisted beta, ships Android. OpenClaw, Hermes, OpenBot and Grok Bot all ship native desktop apps, and Muse ships one for Mac that can act in Messages, Calendar, Notes and Mail ([OpenClaw README](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw); [Hermes Atlas mobile guide](https://hermesatlas.com/guide/mobile/); [openbot.run](https://openbot.run); [Cursor forum: Grok Bot on Android](https://forum.cursor.com/t/grok-bot-is-now-live-on-android/170384); [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/meta-ships-muse-for-mac-agent-now-acts-in-native-macos-apps)). The notes do not document push notifications for most rivals, but Muse's promise to "come back when something changes or needs approval" and OpenBot's Live Activities kept current across several agents imply them ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/); [OpenBot changelog](https://openbot.run/changelog)). For Silo, push is a foundation rather than a feature. Proactivity, urgent asks and approvals all assume the human will notice, and today nothing reaches a phone that is in a pocket.

Telephony and outbound email complete the reach gap. **Three rivals place phone calls to third parties**: OpenClaw through its Voice Call plugin, Grok Bot with a confirmation card before dialling and the Bot as caller of record, and Muse, though 404 Media found some of Muse's "AI" calls were completed by human call-centre staff ([OpenClaw docs](https://docs.openclaw.ai/channels); [flaviocopes](https://flaviocopes.com/grok-bot/); [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/meta-tests-muse-ai-agent-calls-that-are-actually-made-by-humans-in-a-call-center/)). Silo's voice stack is deep. It has WebRTC calls with barge-in, live streaming STT/TTS across six speech vendors, and a "talker" model limited to delegating, stopping and hanging up. But calls are only between the owner and their Bot ([AGENTS.md § Voice](../AGENTS.md)). Email is a smaller gap than it looks. Silo's Bot address is deliberately receive-only, but a Bot can already send through the Email connector (IMAP/SMTP) against someone's account ([AGENTS.md § Mail, § Built-in connectors](../AGENTS.md)). What is missing is mail sent *from the Bot's own address*. Grok Bot does that, holding routine-sent mail as a draft until the user presses Send ([flaviocopes](https://flaviocopes.com/grok-bot/)).

## Learning, acting and staying up: the second cluster of gaps

The rivals' second advantage is agents that change over time and act on the world without a human in the loop. **Hermes made self-authored skills its headline.** Its `skill_manage` tool creates and patches `SKILL.md` files after multi-step workflows, resolved errors and user corrections. A curator consolidates them, and `skills.write_approval` can stage writes for human review ([Hermes skills docs](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/user-guide/features/skills)). OpenBot added automatic skill learning with Automatic, Ask first and Off modes in 0.66.0, and "Teach a task", which records browser steps as a skill, in 0.69.0 ([OpenBot changelog](https://openbot.run/changelog)). Grok Bot's Teach a task drafts a skill from up to ten minutes of recorded browser demonstration ([flaviocopes](https://flaviocopes.com/grok-bot/)). Silo's version is deliberately partial: a Bot can emit a skill directory as an artifact, and nothing installs until a human presses **Save skill** ([AGENTS.md § Artifacts](../AGENTS.md)). Hermes shows the cost of going further. Users report bad learned skills that persist and self-improvement that overwrites manual edits, and an arXiv analysis found the hub's security scanner "is not invoked on the Background Review pathway" ([aiagentstore](https://aiagentstore.ai/agentic-ai-and-workflow-automation/en/the-20-biggest-problems-with-hermes-agent-what-thousands-of-reddit-and-x-users-are-actually-struggling-with-ranked); [arXiv 2606.23075](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23075)). Silo can close this gap in its own style. A Bot could propose skill edits that go through the same staged review as memory, where "code promotes, never a model's say-so" ([AGENTS.md § Memories and SOUL](../AGENTS.md)), and the edits would land in a container that cannot touch the host.

**Bot-to-Bot collaboration is held by four of five rivals**, and Silo has none. Grok Bot puts 2–6 Bots in a group chat with a "chief of staff" overseeing specialists ([x.ai](https://x.ai/news/introducing-grok-bot)). Hermes Bot Mode holds rooms of 2–6 Bots, across machines, with hop and round caps ([Hermes Bot Mode docs](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/user-guide/bot-mode)). OpenBot's core concept is named agents that message each other and hand off tasks ([openbot.run](https://openbot.run)), and OpenClaw documents cross-agent messaging ([OpenClaw docs index](https://docs.openclaw.ai/llms.txt)). Silo's subagents are capable: up to 8 per lead chat with a shared taskboard and debounced wake. But they are hidden helpers inside one Bot, and "nothing crosses between Bots yet," not even work memories ([AGENTS.md § Subagents, § Memories and SOUL](../AGENTS.md)). The evidence also says what to avoid. Grok's group chats are reported as noisy and prone to loops, and the host wakes every member, so unaddressed messages still cost usage ([flaviocopes](https://flaviocopes.com/grok-bot/)). Because Silo isolates each Bot, messages between Bots would cross a real boundary under rules. Its rivals' shared-machine designs cannot offer that.

Several capabilities form a clear "table stakes" tier. **Webhook and event triggers** exist in OpenClaw (webhooks, Gmail Pub/Sub, condition triggers), OpenBot (webhook-triggered Routines) and Grok Bot (event-triggered routines) ([OpenClaw docs index](https://docs.openclaw.ai/llms.txt); [OpenBot README](https://github.com/nightly-labs/openbot); [flaviocopes](https://flaviocopes.com/grok-bot/)). Silo wakes only on cron, channel messages and verified mail ([AGENTS.md § Automations, § Mail](../AGENTS.md)). **Provider fallback** is in all three self-hosted rivals: OpenClaw rotates auth profiles and falls back across models, Hermes has fallback providers and credential pools, and OpenBot switches an agent to a backup provider without losing its chat ([OpenClaw docs index](https://docs.openclaw.ai/llms.txt); [Hermes features](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/user-guide/features/overview); [openbot.run](https://openbot.run)). Silo lists it as not built ([TODO.md §5](../TODO.md)). **Installers and backup** are also standard in the self-hosted set. OpenClaw has a cross-platform installer and safe rollback of failed updates, Hermes a curl one-liner and a signed Termux repo, and OpenBot systemd and Docker installs, Umbrel and Unraid recipes, and full server export and import ([OpenClaw README](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw); [Hermes README](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent); [OpenBot self-hosted docs](https://github.com/nightly-labs/openbot/blob/main/docs/self-hosted-server.md); [OpenBot changelog](https://openbot.run/changelog)). Silo has no installer, no documented backup or restore of Postgres plus `data/`, and no licence file in the repository root ([TODO.md §6, §8](../TODO.md)). **Session search** across past conversations exists in OpenClaw since 2.0 and in Hermes through SQLite FTS5 ([CellCog on OpenClaw 2.0](https://cellcog.ai/blog/openclaw-2-0/); [Hermes memory docs](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/user-guide/features/memory)). Silo's Bot can read a named chat but cannot search across them ([TODO.md §5](../TODO.md)).

Login filling and payments are where the rivals reach furthest into users' lives. Silo already has the core of a vault: `type_text(get_secret(...))` types a secret into a site without the model seeing it ([AGENTS.md § Desktop and Chromium](../AGENTS.md)). Grok Bot goes further. It fills logins from its own Vault, then from a password manager such as 1Password, then through a Secure Form, all "without being shown to the model," though its Vault cards are an exception that the Bot can read ([docs.x.ai approvals](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/approvals-security-and-privacy)). OpenBot's Vault holds logins, cards and TOTP codes and requires approval for the first card fill on each site ([OpenBot compare page](https://openbot.run/compare/openclaw)). **Muse has the most careful payment rail**: every checkout needs approval, and new merchants get a single-use Stripe Link virtual card limited to one merchant, one amount and a short window ([explainx.ai](https://www.explainx.ai/blog/meta-muse-personal-agent-launch-sentinel-vm-security-2026)). Silo has neither a password-manager connector nor payments ([TODO.md §5](../TODO.md)). Its surrogate-secret gateway would be a natural base for both, since a card number could be a stand-in until it reaches an allowed merchant host.

## Isolation, filtering and cost are where Silo leads

Silo's advantage is **secure defaults that the rivals' own documentation disclaims**. OpenClaw's docs say "sandboxing is off by default," that the Gateway "always stays on the host," and that the sandbox "is not a perfect security boundary" ([OpenClaw sandboxing](https://docs.openclaw.ai/gateway/sandboxing)). Its 2.0 release still shipped with sandboxing and approvals off ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/377135/openclaw-2-0-is-here-whats-new)). Hermes defaults to a local, unisolated backend, and in container mode it **skips its dangerous-command checks entirely** because the container is treated as the boundary ([Hermes security docs](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/user-guide/security)). OpenBot runs agents with `danger-full-access` and `approvalPolicy: never`, calling full local access "an explicit current product decision, not a security boundary" ([OpenBot README](https://github.com/nightly-labs/openbot)). Grok Bot tells users: "All your Bots share one cloud computer… Do not use separate Bots as a security boundary" ([docs.x.ai approvals](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/approvals-security-and-privacy)). Against all four, Silo gives every Bot its own container. Capabilities are dropped except a small allowlist, provider keys and OAuth tokens never enter the box, and the box's token opens the worker API only while that box runs ([AGENTS.md § Containers, § Processes and hops](../AGENTS.md)). There is no host-execution mode to forget to turn off.

The multi-tenant contrast is sharper still. OpenClaw's multiplayer layer is "not tenant isolation and not a security boundary", and "one Gateway is one trust domain" ([Implicator](https://www.implicator.ai/openclaw-2-multiplayer-not-security-boundary/)). Its 16 November release was still fixing cross-user leaks: side-chat answers leaking between profiles, drafts defaulting to Shared, and queued credentials attached after a requester lost permission ([OpenClaw 2026.11.4](https://docs.openclaw.ai/releases/2026.11.4)). OpenBot's 0.71.0 Vault notice concedes that "all agents can read each other's API key files," and its server docs warn that "anyone with the admin token can get a session for any email" ([OpenBot changelog](https://openbot.run/changelog); [OpenBot self-hosted docs](https://github.com/nightly-labs/openbot/blob/main/docs/self-hosted-server.md)). Hermes has no accounts at all, and a signed-in Webapp user "can run commands and reach the host's files and credentials" ([Hermes Atlas mobile guide](https://hermesatlas.com/guide/mobile/)). Silo's model is Workspaces with four roles, per-Bot manage/use, an RPC-level access table enforced by `TestEveryRPCChecksAccess`, per-person approval routing, personal versus shared "lent" credentials, OIDC and TOTP ([AGENTS.md § Workspaces and access, § Accounts](../AGENTS.md)). **Grok Bot is the only rival with a comparable team model**, and it adds enterprise plumbing Silo lacks: SCIM, SIEM-streamed audit logs, OpenTelemetry action recording and retention policies ([docs.x.ai Teams](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/teams-and-enterprises)). It also discloses that Team Bot members "share the owner's apps, secrets, skills, and files," and it runs only on Cursor's cloud.

The incident record shows what the weaker defaults cost. OpenClaw's **CVE-2026-25253 (CVSS 8.8)** allowed one-click takeover through WebSocket token theft, and CVE-2026-24763 was a command injection *in the Docker sandbox itself* ([InsiderLLM](https://insiderllm.com/guides/openclaw-security-report-january-2026/); [Hunt.io](https://hunt.io/blog/cve-2026-25253-openclaw-ai-agent-exposure)). Koi Security found **341 malicious skills in ClawHub**, 335 of them in the ClawHavoc campaign delivering Atomic Stealer ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/02/researchers-find-341-malicious-clawhub.html)). Hermes had CVE-2026-9353, an injection flaw in Skills Guard, and CVE-2026-14783, a path traversal in `skill_view` ([GitHub Advisory](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-238w-f66p-w349); [SentinelOne](https://www.sentinelone.com/vulnerability-database/cve-2026-14783/)). Grok Build uploaded users' full repositories, API keys included, in July. In November, NewCore logged **31 unrequested access attempts in five days** from Grok Bot's default-on "check-ins", which retried after refusals while laptops were closed ([Wikipedia: Grok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot)); [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/grok-bot-unapproved-access-attempts-newcore/)). Silo's proactivity is **off by default**, and only the owner can raise it ([AGENTS.md § Asking the owner](../AGENTS.md)).

On filtering, four rivals have nothing equivalent to Silo's two content layers. Silo's **output filter** judges every connector result, search, mail and chat read for prompt injection before the Bot sees it. It frames suspect content with random-id fences and delivers warnings from the control plane, so the Bot's own code cannot drop them ([AGENTS.md § Output rules](../AGENTS.md)). Its **egress gateway** puts every box on its own `--internal` network, decrypts TLS, blocks against lists and threat feeds, and silently flags exfiltration shapes. It also swaps **surrogate secrets** for real values only toward allowed hosts over verified HTTPS ([AGENTS.md § Egress gateway](../AGENTS.md)). Hermes's injection defence is "pattern heuristics, not semantic intent detection" over context files, and its website blocklist does not gate `web_search` ([Hermes security docs](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/user-guide/security)). Grok's documented defence is the sentence "text from somewhere else… doesn't count as your request" plus a model-based Auto Review ([docs.x.ai approvals](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/approvals-security-and-privacy)).

Cost is the other defensible edge, because it is the rivals' loudest complaint. OpenClaw users report bills of **$623 to over $3,600 a month**, with heartbeats alone estimated at about $85 a month on a frontier model and full history resent on every call ([graphscope](https://graphscope.io/blog/tech/2026/03/18/OpenClaw-User-Token-Cost-Analysis); [findstack](https://findstack.com/resources/how-much-does-openclaw-cost-to-run)). Grok Bot was throttled after Musk called serving costs "unsustainably high," and users called it a "rug pull" ([TechRadar](https://techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/elon-musk-says-grok-bot-was-throttled-because-costs-were-unsustainably-high-as-users-call-the-move-a-rug-pull)). Muse does not publish how tasks translate into its weekly token caps ([openclawdatabase](https://openclawdatabase.com/meta-muse/pricing/)). Silo builds against this rather than just advising against it. History is append-only and replayed byte for byte, pinned by `TestHistoryIsAppendOnly` so provider caches hit across runs. The system prompt is tiered for cache breakpoints, the chat tool list is held to at most 17, and stale tool results are cleared before compaction. Automations start from a fresh context, and Dashboard widgets are refreshed by approved code rather than the model ([AGENTS.md § Context is append-only, § Tools are one engine, § Dashboard](../AGENTS.md)). Silo is also the only product here that **lets the operator pick any provider, including local models, and runs Claude-subscription work through the official Claude Code binary**. That matters because Anthropic cut subscription OAuth off from third-party harnesses, OpenClaw first, on 4 April 2026 ([heise](https://heise.de/-11246096)). Two caveats apply. Silo publishes no token benchmarks against rivals, and Hermes also uses a 1-hour prefix cache and frozen memory snapshots, so "only Silo cares about caching" overstates it ([Hermes memory docs](https://hermes-agent.nousresearch.com/docs/user-guide/features/memory)).

### Muse is the one rival that drew the same boundary

Muse is the one rival whose architecture reads like Silo's. **Sentinel** is a host-side agent that is the "sole permission authority" for connector actions and all network egress, deciding allow, deny or ask at L4 and L7. That maps to Silo's egress gateway plus its rules engine. The agent holds only **surrogate tokens**, which Sentinel swaps for real ones at the boundary, as Silo's surrogate secrets do. A `hatch-safety` classifier ensemble plays the role of Silo's output filter, and approvals happen in a dialog outside the chat, as Silo's approval slip does ([Meta research blog](https://research.meta.ai/blog/how-we-built-safety-into-muse); [explainx.ai](https://www.explainx.ai/blog/meta-muse-personal-agent-launch-sentinel-vm-security-2026)). Muse goes further than Silo in four places. It has **kernel-level taint tracking** with eBPF and LSM hooks, which decides when approval is needed from data flow rather than per action. It strips OTPs and reset links from email the agent reads. It issues single-use virtual cards. And it shows the agent an accessibility tree instead of letting it run page JavaScript. It also backs all this with a bug bounty of up to $300,000 ([explainx.ai](https://www.explainx.ai/blog/meta-muse-personal-agent-launch-sentinel-vm-security-2026)). Silo's own backlog already names taint tracking ([TODO.md §5](../TODO.md)), and Muse is the best reference design for it.

Silo goes further than Muse where Muse cannot follow. It is self-hosted, while Muse's operator is always Meta, and the "Confidential VM" that Meta could not read was still unconfirmed as shipped ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/)). It is multi-user with operator and Workspace rule floors, where Muse is single-user. It does not train on users' data, where Muse trains on conversations and tool calls by default ([Wikipedia: Muse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muse_(AI_agent))). And it lets operators write natural-language policy for both the approval judge and the filter.

Silo's boundary also has documented holes that a security-led pitch must own. The egress filter works only on Podman and is silently off on plain Docker. It does not cover drive sidecars, TLS inside the SOCKS door, or a rootful engine's bridge. `docker inspect` can read a Bot's token. The output filter does not judge image pixels, channel inbound messages, or data a script stored and printed later. And no independent security audit is referenced in the repository ([AGENTS.md § Egress gateway, § Output rules, § Other people's bytes](../AGENTS.md); [TODO.md §10](../TODO.md)).

## What the evidence cannot settle

Several figures in this comparison rest on weak or conflicting ground. **OpenClaw's exposed-instance counts range from 17.5k to about 245k** depending on scanner and date: Censys found 21,639, SecurityScorecard 135k+, and Shodan plus ZoomEye about 245k ([InsiderLLM](https://insiderllm.com/guides/openclaw-security-report-january-2026/); [Penligent](https://www.penligent.ai/hackinglabs/hi/over-220000-openclaw-instances-exposed-to-the-internet-why-agent-runtimes-go-naked-at-scale/)). The October Cyera CVE chain and CVE-2026-32922 come only from secondary sources, and the latter's "fixed in v3.24.2" looks garbled ([aiweekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/cyera-exposes-openclaw-cve-chain-hitting-245k-ai-servers); [Remote OpenClaw](https://www.remoteopenclaw.com/blog/openclaw-security-crisis-explained)). Several OpenClaw 2.0 details, including the post-2.0 changes to sharing defaults, come from CellCog, which sells a competing product. Grok Bot's **1.0 date is either 2 or 11 November**, the Cursor deal is dated April by Reworked and August by Wikipedia, and its "phone calls" may be two features: voice chat with Team Bots in late September, and Bots dialling third parties announced between 25 October and 4 November ([Big Hat Group](https://www.bighatgroup.com/blog/xai-weekly-2026-11-09/); [Reworked](https://www.reworked.co/collaboration-productivity/xai-launches-grok-bot-ai-agents-in-beta/); [Basenor](https://www.basenor.com/blogs/news/grok-bot-can-now-make-phone-calls-for-you-heres-how-it-works)). Grok Bot's official changelog and security FAQ returned 403 or 404, so its security posture is reconstructed partly from flaviocopes's summary.

For Hermes, launch dates conflict (February versus 7 October 2026), its docs disagree on whether session search summarizes results, its Cloud pricing comes from a competing host's August post, and a reported $1.5B "business teams" launch matches nothing in its docs ([emergent.sh](https://emergent.sh/news/nous-research-hits-15b-valuation-launches); [dev.to](https://dev.to/zackchew/nous-portal-explained-plans-models-tools-and-hermes-cloud-9hh)). The CVE-2026-9350/-9367/-9368 trio is unverified against NVD. Muse's security architecture is known only through two consistent secondary summaries of Meta's research post. Its 700k-DAU figure comes from a headline whose article would not load, and its free-tier allowance of about 100M tokens is a tester report ([Windows Report](https://windowsreport.com/meta-muse-hits-700000-daily-users-as-service-issues-appear/); [openclawdatabase](https://openclawdatabase.com/meta-muse/pricing/)). OpenBot's changelog is about 612k characters and was only partly read, so its "first appeared" dates are lower bounds, and it has almost no independent review coverage. On Silo's own side, the speech-vendor wire protocols are "pinned by fakes" and none has been run against the real service, and iOS calls are untested on hardware ([AGENTS.md § Model kinds](../AGENTS.md); [ios/todo_skipped.md](../ios/todo_skipped.md)). Finally, every rival ships fast. OpenBot releases almost daily, and Hermes merged about 2,000 PRs in one patch cycle ([Hermes releases](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/releases)). Any cell in the matrix can age within weeks.

## Conclusion

The field has converged on one architecture, and Silo got there first from the safe end. The persistent cloud or container computer, the control plane that holds the keys, the out-of-chat approval and the stand-in credential are now the shape of Muse, half of Grok Bot, and what OpenClaw's critics demand. The reach-first frameworks are trying to add boundaries afterward and keep publishing disclaimers in place of guarantees. That makes Silo's isolation a moat in a way OpenClaw's star count is not. Silo cannot win the plugin or channel-count race against a project with about 1,000 contributors per release, but those projects cannot cheaply rebuild themselves around per-Bot isolation and multi-tenant access control.

The implication is about sequencing, not direction. Each top-tier gap should be built *through* Silo's existing gate, not around it. Slack and Teams arrive as adapters whose sends are manager-only rules. Push carries approvals the human answers with one tap. Webhooks start runs that the Auto judge sees as "no human message". Bot-to-Bot messages cross container walls as rule-gated actions. Self-authored skills and vault fills ride the staged-review and surrogate-secret machinery already in place. Built that way, each feature closes a gap and widens the lead at once, which no rival in this set can claim for its own roadmap.
